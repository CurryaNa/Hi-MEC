1. Primary Colors: Pink, red, white (hypopigmentation), and small foci of dark red-brown crust.
2. Border Appearance: The borders are irregular and ill-defined, blending with atrophic, hypopigmented skin that surrounds the more erythematous components.
3. Shape Symmetry: The lesion is asymmetrical, appearing as an elongated, multicomponent plaque.
4. Surface Texture: The texture is varied, featuring fine scaling over the erythematous areas, atrophy (thin and shiny) in the white areas, and erosion with crusting in a focal area.
5. Structural Features: The lesion consists of two main erythematous components: a larger, scaly plaque and a smaller, inferior papule with central ulceration and crusting. These are situated within a larger patch of atrophy and hypopigmentation. Fine telangiectasias are visible, especially within the pink-red areas.
