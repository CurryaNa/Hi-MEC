1. Primary Colors: Erythematous (red), violaceous, yellow (serous crust), and dark red to black (hemorrhagic crust).
2. Border Appearance: The overall inflammatory area has diffuse, ill-defined borders. Individual erosions and ulcerations have more sharply demarcated, irregular margins.
3. Shape Symmetry: Highly asymmetrical, irregularly distributed across the conchal bowl and earlobe.
4. Surface Texture: Polymorphous, featuring moist, weeping surfaces, areas of honey-colored and hemorrhagic crusting, and surrounding edematous skin.
5. Structural Features: Multiple erosions, superficial ulcerations, and a prominent fissure on the earlobe are visible. There is significant serosanguinous exudate and crust formation. The entire affected area appears inflamed and swollen (edematous).
