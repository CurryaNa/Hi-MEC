1. Primary Colors: The lesion displays multiple colors, including light brown, dark brown/black, and whitish-grey areas suggestive of regression.
2. Border Appearance: The borders are highly irregular and poorly defined, with a prominent pseudopod-like extension at the superior aspect.
3. Shape Symmetry: The lesion is markedly asymmetrical in both its contour and the distribution of internal pigment and structures.
4. Surface Texture: The surface appears generally smooth, without significant scale, crusting, or ulceration.
5. Structural Features: Dermoscopic structures include an atypical pigment network, central blotches, areas of scar-like depigmentation (regression), and radial streaming. Fine arborizing telangiectasias are visible in the surrounding skin.
