1. Primary Colors: The lesion displays multiple colors (polychromasia), including prominent bright pink and red, with scattered dark brown to black pigmented globules and whitish, scar-like zones.
2. Border Appearance: The borders are highly irregular and indistinct, with a moth-eaten appearance where the lesion fades into the surrounding skin.
3. Shape Symmetry: The lesion is grossly asymmetrical in both its overall contour and the internal distribution of colors and structures.
4. Surface Texture: The surface is complex, featuring a central, glistening ulceration with evidence of bleeding, surrounded by areas that appear both scaly and atrophic (thinned skin).
5. Structural Features: Prominent features include a central ulcer, multiple asymmetric dark pigment globules and clods, and whitish, structureless areas consistent with regression.
