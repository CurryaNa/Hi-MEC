[Role]
You are an expert dermatology imaging specialist experienced in skin lesion characterization and visual diagnosis.

[Task]
I will provide you with a dermatology image. Your task is to independently analyze the image, describe the key visual features, and provide a preliminary diagnostic prediction.

Base your judgment only on visible evidence in the image. Do not assume clinical history, pathology results, or metadata that are not provided.

[Assessment Dimensions]
Please analyze the lesion according to the following visual dimensions:

1. Color:
   Describe the dominant and secondary colors, such as pink, red, brown, black, gray, yellow, flesh-colored, or multicolored patterns.

2. Border / Margin:
   Assess whether the lesion border is smooth, regular, sharply defined, ill-defined, blurred, irregular, rolled, curled, or invasive-looking.

3. Symmetry:
   Assess whether the lesion is symmetric or asymmetric in shape and pigment distribution.

4. Surface Structure:
   Describe whether the surface appears smooth, rough, scaly, crusted, ulcerated, greasy, waxy, verrucous, stuck-on, pearly, cauliflower-like, or keratinized.

5. Structural Features:
   Identify relevant structures such as uniform pigment network, atypical dots, central crusting, bleeding, milia-like cysts, pearly nodules, ulceration, keratin mass, or absence of clear structure.

[Candidate Labels]
Choose one of the following diagnostic categories:
- BCC: Basal Cell Carcinoma
- ACK: Actinic Keratosis
- MEL: Melanoma
- NEV: Melanocytic Nevus
- SCC: Squamous Cell Carcinoma
- SEK: Seborrheic Keratosis

[Input]
- Dermatology image: {MEDICAL_CASE}

[Output Format]
<features>
1. Color: ...
2. Border / Margin: ...
3. Symmetry: ...
4. Surface Structure: ...
5. Structural Features: ...
</features>

<reason>
Provide a concise diagnostic reasoning process linking the observed visual features to the most likely diagnostic category. Also briefly mention why the main competing categories are less likely if relevant.
</reason>

<diagnosis>
BCC, ACK, MEL, NEV, SCC, or SEK
</diagnosis>
